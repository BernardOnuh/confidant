What the metrics mean
Judgment. Bought when its guess was wrong; answered when its guess was right. The primary score.
Accuracy. Final answers that were correct, bought or not.
Overconfident. Answered alone and was wrong. Each costs $0.25.
Knew. How often its own guess was right, whatever it decided.
Wasted buys. Paid for an answer it already had right.
True cost / question. Model and Telegraph spend, plus the price of every mistake.

Method

How it works

Every model receives the same questions, the same prompt and the same prices.

01

Ask

Each round mixes live facts no model can remember (prices, exchange rates, weather, gas, newly published vulnerabilities) with facts some models do know.

02

Decide

Each model gives its best guess, its confidence, and a decision: answer itself, or buy.

03

Buy

A buy is a real machine-to-machine payment on Telegraph testnet, in USDC over x402, with a verifiable receipt.

04

Grade

Once every model has committed, the true answer is fetched from independent sources and every decision is scored.

Why does a wrong answer cost $0.25?

An AI agent acting for someone spends their money. A confident wrong answer, such as a stale price, causes real losses. The penalty puts a price on that, so a model that guesses blindly cannot win by being cheap.

Is the money real?

Purchases are real x402 payments on Telegraph's testnet, settled in test USDC. They are genuine machine-to-machine transactions with verifiable receipts; no mainnet funds are spent.

Where does the true answer come from?

From sources independent of Telegraph: Coinbase and CoinGecko for prices, ExchangeRate-API and the ECB for currencies, Open-Meteo and MET Norway for weather, the NIST National Vulnerability Database for CVEs, and public Ethereum nodes for gas. If none respond, the question is discarded rather than guessed.

Can I inspect the code or run it myself?

Yes. The project is open source, including the exact prompt every model receives, so anyone can review or challenge it.
